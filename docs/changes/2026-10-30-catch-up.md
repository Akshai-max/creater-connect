# `npm run catch-up`: work through the backlog of almost-qualified leads

**Date:** 2026-10-30
**Files:** `scripts/catch-up.ts` (new), `package.json`

## Why
You asked for 1,000 leads today, as fast as possible. On the morning of 2026-10-30 the engine had 4,286 leads:

| | Leads |
|---|---|
| qualified | 38 |
| unverified (evidence missing) | 1,326 |
| rejected (wrong country, inactive, agency…) | 2,922 |

What the unverified leads were missing (a lead can miss several):

| Missing | Leads |
|---|---|
| a contact route (no published email) | 1,157 |
| the entity check (individual creator not confirmed) | 995 |
| monetization evidence | 663 |
| country | 477 |
| need evidence | 453 |

**1,000 *qualified* today isn't reachable** without breaking the rules: no guessed or bought contacts, and no creator counted as an individual without their own words saying so. More discovery doesn't change that quickly either. About 1% of discovered channels qualify, and searches are capped at 100 a day.

**The two biggest blockers can be worked through today with real evidence.** The 20-minute cycle does only 25 website contact lookups and 150 entity checks a run, so most of the backlog waits.

## What changed
`npm run catch-up` runs once over the whole backlog:
1. **Contacts:** the website contact finder over up to 2,000 leads, for up to 150 minutes (`--contacts`, `--contact-minutes`). The rules are the same as in the cycle: the creator's own site, `robots.txt` honoured, only emails published on the page, 1 YouTube unit a lead.
2. **Entity check:** up to 1,200 model calls (`--entity`) through Groq and Gemini, with the same exact-quote rule. Leads that just got a contact now count as "entity decides", so they're checked too.
3. **Engine:** reruns the engine on the updated file, so the Leads page shows the result.

It holds the cycle's lock and refreshes it every 5 minutes, so scheduled cycles skip while it runs instead of writing the lead file at the same time. If a cycle is running when it starts, it waits for it.

## Result
**First run** (contacts plus a title-only entity check, 13 min):
- **Contacts:** 184 leads checked (the rest were rejected for other reasons or checked within 30 days); 30 emails found, 24 marked business; 184 YouTube units.
- **Entity check:** all 1,022 came from the cache as "can't confirm". Every one of the 24 leads that got an email was still blocked by the entity check.
- **Qualified:** 38 → 39.

**Second run** (entity check with video descriptions, `--with-descriptions`, 16 min):
- **The first attempt stopped** after 1 call, because the router had parked Groq and Gemini until 07:00 UTC on a guessed time; see [provider values, not guesses](2026-10-30-provider-values-not-guesses.md). After that fix it ran fully.
- **Descriptions** were fetched for 83 leads that needed only the entity check (168 YouTube units).
- **82 model calls:** 8 confirmed individual, 5 excluded.
- **Qualified: 39 → 50** (unverified 1,445, rejected 3,162, 4,657 leads in total including new discovery).

**Why not 1,000 today:** of 3,918 unconfirmed leads, only 65 (later 83) would qualify even if confirmed; the rest fail activity, country, monetization or need. The numbers grow with more discovery (each fresh YouTube project brings 3,000–4,500 channels, and about 1–2% qualify) and with you confirming creators by hand on the lead page.
